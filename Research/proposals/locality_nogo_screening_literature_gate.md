## The vein

The site's novelty claim, as currently worded on `/for-researchers`, is that prior work tested
acceleration against radius, orbital frequency, size, and surface density, but **never
volumetric ρ(r) explicitly**. Against that stands an entire subfield of local-matter-density-keyed
gravity modification:

- **Symmetron** (Hinterbichler & Khoury, PRL 104, 231301, 2010) — a scalar whose Z₂ symmetry is
  *restored above a critical local matter density*. It has an action, a genuine order parameter,
  a real symmetry-breaking phase transition, and a tanh-like field profile. It is, structurally,
  the field-theoretic object the framework has been hand-building without a Lagrangian.
- **Chameleon** (Khoury & Weltman, 2004) — thin-shell condition keyed on local density/potential.
- **Dedicated observational tests of density-environment-dependent gravity already exist**:
  Jain, Vikram & Sakstein (2013) on unscreened void dwarfs; Desmond & Ferreira (2020) on
  ~24,000 galaxies.

## What survives and what does not

I do **not** think this refutes the 07-23 gate, and the proposal should not be read that way.
The gate cleared a narrow, specific claim: *no published quantification of volumetric ρ(r)
failing as the MOND organizing variable*. Symmetron does not attempt to reproduce the RAR;
it is a screening mechanism for a fifth force, and its critical density does a different job.
That distinction is real and probably defensible.

What does **not** survive is the *wording*. As written, the site says local-density
modification is "excluded." Symmetron and chameleon models are local-density-keyed,
action-based, and alive. A referee reads "excluded," produces symmetron in one line, and
stops reading — and the narrow claim that would have survived never gets adjudicated.

**Proposed scoping**, which is narrower, defensible, and more interesting than the current form:

> Local volumetric density cannot reproduce the RAR/BTFR, because those relations are
> organized in acceleration and a density key introduces cross-system scatter of ~1.7 dex.

This is, incidentally, close to the conclusion the screening community reached by a different
route, and is plausibly *why* nobody keyed a MOND substitute on ρ after 1983 — which makes the
honest framing "we quantified a known reason" rather than "we asked an unasked question."
That is a smaller claim. It is also one that survives review.

## The recursive item, which is the sharper one

The program's methodology sector reports a **vocabulary-asymmetry test with a 4/4 catch rate on
prior-art rediscovery after notation translation**. That test has never been run on artifact #1.

The framework's flagship surviving contribution is the one claim that has never been through
the framework's own working detector.

This is not irony for its own sake — it is a concrete, cheap, high-yield action. The detector's
one demonstrated competence is exactly the failure mode at issue here (a claim that looks novel
in its own notation and turns out to be prior art in the field's notation). Running it on the
no-go is the single highest-information experiment available in the methodology sector, because
it tests the detector *and* the artifact simultaneously, and both outcomes are informative:

- Detector fires ⇒ the no-go's novelty claim needs the scoping above, and the detector earns a
  genuine out-of-sample catch (its first).
- Detector stays silent ⇒ either the no-go is novel as scoped, or the detector's 4/4 was
  corpus-specific. Disambiguate by also running it on symmetron-as-translated-into-framework-
  vocabulary as a positive control.
