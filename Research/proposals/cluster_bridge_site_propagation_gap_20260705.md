## What happened

The cluster-scale bridge problem (C(ρ) → apparent cluster mass) was closed by execution on
2026-05-28: four natural ansätze tested against Coma, all fail (two by 10⁴ overshoot, one
collapses to Newtonian, one is structurally bounded at ≤2 vs. observed 4.6). Refined 2026-06-01
(root cause is wrong variable — local ρ vs. non-local g_bar — not one-scale insufficiency) and
2026-06-09 (the no-go is a quantified instance of Milgrom's non-locality theorem, not a novel
Synchronism result). This closure was correctly propagated to `/for-researchers` and
`/honest-assessment`.

It was **not** propagated to `/tier-1-existing`, whose CLUSTER-SCALE entry was last edited
2026-05-24 — four days *before* the closure — and still read "the cluster-scale bridge (if it
exists in the archive) is the last open door for the physics program," i.e., presented as an
open question rather than a closed, executed, failed result. This entry stood unedited for
over five weeks after being superseded by the site's own other pages.

**Independent confirmation:** the 2026-07-05 visitor Pass 4 (researcher persona, WebFetch only,
no access to explorer findings) visited `/tier-1-existing` directly, read the stale entry, and
concluded "no cluster test anywhere on the public roadmap," adding: "my strong suspicion... is
that the cluster case was worked and failed... but if so, it belongs on the public roadmap as a
badged failure, not omitted." The persona's suspicion was exactly correct — the work existed,
correctly stated elsewhere on the same site, just not on the one page a researcher checking the
Tier 1 test roadmap would land on.

## Process note — this is the 4th recorded instance, and it's a new sub-pattern

Prior instances (LIV "refuted", TEST-04a direction, CDM σ_int "below CDM") were each a single
stale page carrying an outdated verdict. This instance is different in kind: the *correct*
finding **was** propagated — to two of three relevant pages. The propagation gap is not binary
(updated vs. not updated); it can be **partial across pages describing the same claim**, and the
page that got missed was specifically the one a researcher-persona would check first for "is
there a discriminating test" (the numbered Tier 1 test roadmap), while the pages that got
updated (`/for-researchers`, `/honest-assessment`) are the ones a maintainer session is more
likely to be actively editing when closing a finding. Propagation gaps appear to concentrate on
peripheral/list-style pages that aren't the natural target of the session that produced the fix,
not on the primary narrative pages.

**Implication for the loop:** a "did this reach every page that discusses this claim" grep sweep
(by topic keyword, not just by page) should be a standard last step when closing any Tier-1/
cluster/mechanism-class finding — the same discipline the 2026-07-04 citation-walk audit applied
to site-vs-archive consistency, applied instead to site-internal cross-page consistency. Seeded
to the explorer track as a topic (`site-propagation-completeness-audit.md`).
