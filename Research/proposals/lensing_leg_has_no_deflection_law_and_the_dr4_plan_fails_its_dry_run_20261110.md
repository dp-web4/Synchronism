## 1. The strongest dataset against rows #1–#2 is conditional on a rule the framework does not have

Since 2026-09-25 the site has used the KiDS-1000 weak-lensing RAR (Brouwer et al. 2021) as the "independent leg" of TEST-10 and as the data answer to the TEST-09 ceiling-convention question ("excludes every candidate ceiling, 6.39 included"). The 11-05 ledger block names it "the strongest independent dataset against the boost cap".

A researcher persona pointed out what `/for-researchers` already states in its own words: **no light-deflection rule exists** in this framework. Lensing measures the deflection of light; rotation curves measure the dynamical potential. Reading a lensing g_obs as a dynamical boost assumes the two coincide. In AeST and TeVeS that holds by construction (the physical metric couples to light and matter alike). Here it is undefined: the framework has a C(ρ) or C(g) that multiplies G in a modified-Poisson equation and no statement about photons.

So the lensing leg is a **conditional** exclusion: *if* lensing traces the same effective potential that moves stars, every Ω_m-based cap is excluded at g_bar ≲ 10⁻¹⁴ m/s². That is the natural reading (it is what every modified-Poisson theory with a conformally coupled metric gives), and it is the reading the site adopted without saying so. The card, the ledger note and the Roadmap now print the assumption. What this changes:

- The TEST-09 convention question is **not** settled by lensing alone. It is settled only under the assumption, which the framework would have to earn by writing a deflection rule. The site stops using the leg as the settlement and prints it as "settled, conditional on a deflection rule the framework has not written".
- TEST-10's rotation-curve leg (three discs above the most permissive cap at ≥ 3σ, census-conditional) does not depend on this and stands as before.

**The ×5 allowance, sourced today.** The site's "every cosmic baryon inside the lensing radius (×5)" was my own allowance on 09-25, not a number from Brouwer et al. I read the paper today (arXiv HTML v1). Verbatim, Sec. 1 footnote 3: "We note that this value of g_bar only takes into account the stellar and cold gas mass of the galaxy." So the persona's suggestion that the fiducial g_bar already includes hot gas, and that ×5 double-counts, is **refuted for the fiducial**: hot gas enters only as a sensitivity case (their Sec. 5.2, isothermal within 100 h⁻¹ kpc, gas-to-stellar fraction 1; read in summary, not verbatim). But the same footnote continues: "the contributions of additional hot gas, dust and 'missing baryons' could increase this value to g_bar ≈ 10⁻¹⁴ m s⁻²", i.e. about an order of magnitude at their outer edge. Brouwer's own most generous allowance is therefore larger than the site's ×5. At ×10 with the 0.3 dex allowance, the required boost at 10⁻¹⁴ drops from 11 to about 5.5, **below** the most permissive cap 6.39. The explorer's 10-04 "census-conditional" verdict already said the exclusion holds only if hidden baryons are < 2–3× stars + cold gas; this is the same condition from the paper's side. Explorer topic seeded to re-run `lensing_ceiling_every_convention.py` at f = 10 and to read Sec. 4.3 and 5.2 verbatim.

**Net:** the lensing leg has two conditions, both now printed: a deflection rule, and a baryon census. Neither is a rescue; the caps still fail at 10⁻¹⁵, and the framework as written (1/Ω_m = 3.17) fails on rotation curves alone. But "the strongest independent dataset" is not unconditional, and it should not be the thing that decides whether row #1 counts.

**For the project lead:** does the framework commit to light following the effective potential? If yes, say so in FUNDAMENTALS (one sentence) and the leg becomes unconditional. If not, the lensing leg is a statement about AeST-like completions, not about this framework.

## 2. The DR4 plan cannot be adopted as written; the protocol it pointed to is withdrawn

The 11-09 proposal asked you to adopt `synchronism-site/maintainer/scripts/wb_dr4_analysis_plan_PREREG.md` by about 25 November. The explorer dry-ran it on DR3 (PREREG `c31d20e` before any script). Four defects, each with a fix the explorer tested:

1. **The calibration bin (g_N > 3×10⁻¹⁰ m/s²) is a MOND regime.** An EFE-free law predicts 1.2–1.4 there; the EFE taper gives ≈ 1.1. On mocks the "calibration fails" rule fires for 55 % of EFE-free catalogues at N = 150 and 100 % at N = 500. Chae et al. treat Newton as verified only above 10⁻⁸ m/s². Fix: calibrate at g_N ≥ 10⁻⁸ (Chae's high-acceleration sample) or at s = 0.2–2 kau.
2. **The calibration rule cannot pass on large 2D samples.** The e-prior systematic is ≈ ±5 %, far above the statistical error at N ≈ 36,000 (the > 3 bin reads 1.036–1.047 ± 0.008, 4–6σ from 1 under any prior). Fix: a stated systematic tolerance, or read the low bins as a ratio to calibration.
3. **The outcome table has no row for a boost above 1.5.** EFE-free predicts ≈ 2.6 on S1's g distribution, so the table could not have recorded the outcome it was built to detect. Fix: add the row.
4. **The 3D instrument cannot reach S2.** `wb_estimators.py` needs ΔRV to ≈ 100 m/s; in Chae's 81,880-pair DR3 catalogue no pair has σ(ΔRV) < 0.1 km/s (median 10 km/s). DR4 improves S1's σ(ln γ) from 0.237 to 0.235. Fix: a 2D estimator for S2.

Run on DR3 as written, the plan returns "calibration bin fails → no result" on both samples. **Recommendation: do not adopt; adopt the revised version the explorer specifies, or leave it a dated draft.** The reading key (boost → γ) is unaffected and can still be adopted on its own.

**Boufourou 2026 (arXiv:2608.24556) is withdrawn by its author.** The explorer found, and I confirmed today by reading the repository, that `HBoufourou/paperI-wide-binaries` `CORRECTION.md` (signed 8 September 2026) states: "The affected observational measurements and the gravity-exclusion claims based on them are withdrawn." The 81,088-pair input was Chae's Newtonian mock (Zenodo 10652994); the explorer reproduced the paper's 41,760-pair fiducial count exactly on the mock file. The arXiv listing shows v1 only, no notice, as of today. The site cited its γ = 1.045 and "rejects 1.4 at ~16σ" as a DR3 measurement on `/wide-binaries` and listed it among the pro-Newton analyses; the 11-09 proposal and the plan pointed to its DR4 protocol as a candidate to adopt. All three are corrected today. A researcher persona had asked for exactly this check ("I would check the author's repository for a correction before December") the day after the explorer did it.

**The S1 crux, restated as a number.** With a physical hidden-companion model (photocentre orbits through RUWE, CMD, IPD and RV-stability cuts), a per-star close-companion rate F = 0.3 leaves only ≈ 7 % of the 36 contaminated, yet moves the mixture estimate of γ from 1.87 to about 1.2 [1.0, 1.6]. Explaining the number of systems near escape speed with Newton needs F ≈ 0.45–0.5, which is the field multiplicity of solar-type stars (Moe & Di Stefano 2017: 0.50 ± 0.04) but 3–5× what the 2D parent sample's calibration-bin level implies under Chae's e prior. So S1 needs either a boost or an unusually contaminated sample; the per-star companion rate of these 36 is measurable (RV monitoring, DR4 epoch astrometry). If Saad & Ting or Chae et al. are contacted, that is the useful content. The 11-09 status sentence "γ ≈ 1.5–1.8 if f ≤ 0.1" came from the 11-08 log-uniform placeholder kick and is replaced.
